# RIEVA missionwear 3D asset

`rieva-missionwear.glb` is generated in Blender by
[`scripts/build_rieva_suits.py`](../../scripts/build_rieva_suits.py). The three
suit concepts, equipment geometry, materials, hierarchy, and interaction
metadata are original work for this project.

The anatomical scaffold is derived from **Human Base Meshes v1.4.1**, published
by Blender Studio and community contributors under CC0. The source bundle was
downloaded from Blender's official Demo Files page and verified on 1 October
2026. CC0 does not require attribution, but it is recorded here for provenance.

- Source: <https://www.blender.org/download/demo-files/>
- Direct bundle listing: `Human Base Meshes v1.4.1`; the downloaded archive
  contents are dated 20 January 2026
- License shown by Blender: CC0
- Source ZIP SHA-256:
  `811f43accbb31a88266d932f8f5563b2d13586fca0ba2693aad1f5fe582b3515`

The GLB stores only the generated browser asset, not the full 49 MB source
library. Each interactive item carries `part_id`, `materialRole`, and concept
metadata as glTF extras.

The current **soft-goods tailoring R3** pass adds restrained pressure-garment
volume, low-relief quilting/ease folds, body-following restraint webbing,
original chest service hardware, articulated glove guards, shaped boot vamps
and replaceable tread lugs, a continuous rounded visor frame and secondary
gasket, an optional external helmet equipment rail, twin service-hose routing,
and a more integrated life-support pack. MAX and MALTE remain surface-EVA
concepts with pressure enclosure and life support; BASTIAN remains an open-head
Earth mobility garment and is explicitly tagged as not suitable for vacuum.
MALTE's authored default uses a continuous signal-orange soft-goods shell with
graphite articulation and interfaces; runtime palettes can replace it. The
optional external helmet pods remain available as named configurator parts but
are hidden in the neutral Blender preview.

The browser export is 5,567,232 bytes and contains 431 named meshes, 162,921
exported primitive vertices, and 228,342 triangles. It also contains 94
selectable part nodes, 27 articulation pivots, 431 material-role nodes, and 174
construction-metadata nodes.

The user supplied a CGTrader Orange ACES listing as a visual quality reference.
Its model, meshes, textures, files, and branding were **not downloaded,
imported, traced, or redistributed**. The public listing was consulted only for
generic soft-goods construction themes such as controlled joint folds, dark
cuff seals, restraint routing, and visible service connections; every RIEVA
surface and layout is independently generated by the script above.

- Comparative listing: <https://www.cgtrader.com/free-3d-models/character/sci-fi-character/spacesuit-orange-aces-features-and-specifications>
- Listing author: `ismaelcampos2000`; model ID `5977872`; published 20 March
  2025; listed as Royalty Free License (no AI)
- Visual-reference check: 19 October 2026
- Operational context: NASA, [Orion Suit Equipped to Expect the Unexpected on
  Artemis Missions](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/orion-suit-equipped-to-expect-the-unexpected-on-artemis-missions/).
  NASA distinguishes the orange Orion launch/entry garment from a surface EVA
  suit; RIEVA preserves that distinction and does not present visual similarity
  as shared qualification or function.

## RIEVA AERIS-X aircraft

`rieva-aeris-x.glb` is generated by
[`scripts/build_aeris_x.py`](../../scripts/build_aeris_x.py). It is an original,
unarmed cranked-delta atmospheric research demonstrator: the fuselage and
canopy are authored lofts, the wings use generated airfoil sections, the
inlets have shaped internal ducts, and the two engine paths include modeled
fan blades and lobed exhaust nozzles. Selectable parts and optional research
modules are separate named meshes/groups with glTF metadata. Internal cutaway
content includes the toggleable `MODULE_EM_HARDENING` faceted avionics shield
and two separately routed luminous trunks under `MODULE_REDUNDANT_CONTROLS`.

The current **R3 fighter-proportion fidelity pass** retains the continuous
airfoil-lofted centerbody/wing transition while tightening the span-to-length
ratio to approximately 0.67, moving the wing crank aft, and adding clipped tips
and restrained chordwise panel seams. It also includes raised forebody chines,
metallic leading-edge caps, separate airfoil-shaped elevons, canted-tail edge
and rudder seams, inlet compression ramps and splitters, multi-stage fan/stator
detail, cowled engine shoulders, lined petalled nozzles, a framed transparent
tandem canopy with cockpit furniture, flush service panels, and modeled landing
doors. Behind those closed cruise doors are original stowed wheels, hubs, oleo
struts, and side braces for cutaway inspection. Materials expose glTF-friendly
metalness, roughness, clear-coat, transmission, anisotropy, and emissive values.
The browser export contains 170 meshes, 11,642 vertices, and 20,124 triangles in
545,340 bytes; the named interactive roots and optional-module hierarchy are
unchanged.

The public preview for Ankur Chauhan's **Fighter Jet** listing on BlendKit was
used only as a visual-quality benchmark for generic aircraft cues: a compact
twin-engine aft volume, faceted blended forebody, side inlets, cranked
trapezoidal wings, canted twin tails, and subtle PBR panel variation. The Full
Plan model, mesh, texture files, and downloadable asset were **not downloaded,
imported, traced, or redistributed**. AERIS-X remains independently generated,
white/chrome-themed, unarmed, and recognizably different in planform and detail.

- Comparative listing: <https://www.blendkit.com/asset-gallery-detail/e4c26dbb-52c9-4c77-9da4-8b7f4f7b4d31/>
- Listing author: Ankur Chauhan; asset title `Fighter Jet`; public page describes
  controllable aircraft surfaces and marks the asset as Full Plan
- Visual-reference check: 19 October 2026

NASA Aeronautics' X-59 educational STL was imported during development as a
real-aircraft proportion and silhouette reference, measured by the build
script, and removed before export. No NASA geometry, livery, name, or logo is
included in AERIS-X. NASA describes its 3D resources as free to download and
use; its GitHub collection describes them as free and without copyright. This
independent project does not imply NASA endorsement.

- Official source page: <https://www.nasa.gov/stem-content/x-59-3d-printing/>
- Source archive: <https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/x59-no-stand.zip>
- NASA 3D usage guidance: <https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/>
- Source author/origin: NASA Aeronautics Research Mission Directorate; Zachary
  Gwennap and Liam Brinton (NASA interns), added 17 December 2021
- Provenance verified: 12 October 2026
- ZIP SHA-256: `7f7bf9cb5f8213d189876bef29d80b5af3aa9a0204a77bf0afbba9cfc2ec6612`
- STL SHA-256: `243f2f2e9e1e63c3aa1fc9ed04937e1c8a9374f33f9bd31ac8099be4f6a29603`

Rebuild after downloading and extracting the reference archive:

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b \
  -P scripts/build_aeris_x.py -- \
  --reference "/path/to/x59-No Stand.STL" \
  --out public/models/rieva-aeris-x.glb
```

The source reference is optional: omit `--reference` to reproduce the authored
AERIS-X geometry without importing the NASA file. The result is concept art
for an educational interface, not an aerodynamic certification model.
